Why I'm excited about CodeAnt

So, here are some ideas

Four ideas I kept coming back to.

Happy to go deeper on any of them.

01

What if CodeAnt had a character?

Say hi to Patch, the ant who finds the cracks.

I keep thinking about how Duolingo built so much of its brand around Duo, the owl. Duo shows up everywhere: in the app, on YouTube, in memes, even at events. People follow Duo, and the product comes along for the ride.

CodeAnt already has a character hiding in its name.

Meet Patch

Tiny. Tireless. A little paranoid.

The one who spots the hole everyone else missed.

  • Patch the detective walks people through each CVE story.
  • Patch the explainer makes pentests and attack surfaces simple, using doors, locks and windows.
  • Patch the nudge: "It's been 7 days since your last scan. Patch is a bit worried."
  • Patch in real life: stickers, merch, maybe a plush ant at meetups.
02

Let people see how CodeAnt gets built

The everyday work is the content.

Some of my favourite SaaS channels right now don't just show features. They show the work behind them.

For CodeAnt, it could look like this

  • The research notebook: how the team found a CVE, from first hunch to disclosure.
  • How an engineer uses CodeAnt: a real day of reviews and scans.
  • More customer stories, like the HockeyStack one.
  • Changelog in 60 seconds for every release.
  • Patch goes hunting: a light, adventure-style series exploring well-known open-source projects.

One video a week, many places

Cut into a few reels and Shorts, and shared on LinkedIn too, where people already follow CodeAnt.

03

Turn the research into a way in

Every finding could bring in new readers, and maybe new users.

The research is honestly the best thing CodeAnt has to show. A few small additions could help it travel further:

  • A simple sign-up: "Get every new CVE we find, in your inbox."
  • One complete library of all the disclosures, easy to browse by software or severity.
  • A gentle next step under each finding: "Curious if your code has this? Try a free scan." It would link to the free tier on the pricing page.
  • One finding, many formats: a carousel, a thread, a Short, a Reddit post. The Camunda finding would be a great one to start with.

04

Make security feel simple on Instagram

Stories first, features second.
  • Found it: a real CVE in 45 seconds, told by Patch.
  • Security, explained like you're 10: a pentest is a friendly burglar checking your locks.
  • Breach of the week: a well-known public breach, explained in five simple slides.
  • Creator collabs: a tech creator tries "I let CodeAnt scan my side project." CrowdStrike did something similar with a security creator.
  • Behind the scenes: the team, the research and the hiring.
